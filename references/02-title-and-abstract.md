# Title and abstract

## Title
- Prefer a statement of the claim over a topic label. Tension forms ("X is not Y") or question forms work when the body can back the promise; a hook in the main title and the searchable terms in a subtitle.
- Checklist: at most 15 words / 100 characters; at least two search keywords; no "novel", "a new", "study of"; every part of the body supports the promise; the one-sentence claim can be recovered from the title alone; no clash with abstract sentences 1 and 4; still accurate in ten years (no tie to a volatile benchmark name).
- Search test: put the candidate words into a scholar search and check that the neighbours returned are the right ones.

## Abstract, 140-200 words
Eight-sentence skeleton: S1 field state -> S2 tension -> S3 root cause -> S4 contribution (named) -> S5 mechanism -> S6 headline number (one group, with protocol) -> S7 second evidence -> S8 boundary + significance + evidence level.
Checklist: no undefined abbreviation; the background does not outweigh the gap; the gap is judgeable by an outsider; concrete numbers, at most two groups (more go to the intro); no repeated sentence; one storyline; self-contained. First-pass test: someone who reads only title + abstract + conclusion can restate the claim, the numbers and the boundary.
Cut order when over budget: background -> secondary numbers -> adjectives. Never cut the protocol qualifier of the headline number.

## Patterns and templates
Title types by paper archetype: name type ("Name: Full Expansion", capability/system papers), claim type ("X yields Y"), tension type ("X is not Y", mechanism-explanation papers, first choice), question type ("Why X ...: mechanism subtitle"). Build it: one complete description sentence from abstract S3 + S4 -> choose type -> delete until keyword density is highest -> add colon structure only if a hook is needed (hook in the main title, searchable terms in the subtitle) -> optional name. Short titles are cited more; purely playful titles are cited less, so pair a hook with a descriptive subtitle.
Abstract principles: a miniature complete story that stands alone; one core contribution; the tension/gap sentence is the engine; results are concrete (headline number with protocol and a comparison anchor); readers know less than you think (the first sentence assumes a wide audience); at least three drafts, written from the body back; last sentence calibrates the evidence level.
Archetype decides the result position: capability papers sell absolute numbers and efficiency; paradigm papers sell a principled gap plus relative gain; mechanism papers sell the named phenomenon and the shape of the law (zero SOTA claims is fine). Capability and paradigm abstracts may skip the field background (proposal-first: "We present X"); mechanism abstracts keep the full funnel.
Anti-patterns: trailer abstracts ("this paper will discuss"), background outweighing the gap, vague claim words without numbers, undefined abbreviations, copying the conclusion sentence by sentence.
