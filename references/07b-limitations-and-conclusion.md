# Limitations and conclusion

## Limitations / scope
- Split by type. **Scope** items (more models, more tasks, real robot, other conditions) are written as next stages; **validity** items (selection history, shared references, metrics that disagree) are calibrated in place in the main text with a clause, and the limitations section at most points to them.
- 3 +/- 1 items, each with limitation -> consequence -> next step. No self-negating subjects ("Our paper fails to"); speculation carries "likely"/"possibly"; scope words match the abstract and body; no fatal-flaw item; no trivial item; removing the section leaves the body self-consistent.

## Conclusion, 100-150 words
- Re-state the finding as a changed claim, not a copy of the abstract: no run of 8 identical words; past tense ("we showed"); no method detail; at most two numbers, each with its protocol; an elevated, normative takeaway; a concrete next-stage sentence that does not repeat the limitations (omit it only if the author decides so); no new information.
- Promise-delivery check: everything the abstract promises is picked up here.

## Patterns and templates
Sentence library (limitations): scoping ("We deliberately scope our claims to <regime>; extending to <Y> is a natural next stage and would require <Z>."); resource ("<Extension> requires <resource> beyond this submission's budget; we consider it the immediate next step."); phenomenon + conjecture ("<observable failure tendency>; this is likely to be addressed by <fix>, though we have not tested it here."); open question ("<X> remains an open research question."). Banned: "Our paper fails to / cannot / does not ...". Rewrite as: "Our evaluation covers <A>; <B> is left to future work."
Sorting flow: list all known limitations -> split scope vs validity -> confirm every validity item is already calibrated in the body, leave at most one pointer -> rank scope items by what a reviewer would catch and keep 3 +/- 1. Over-apology hurts more than the limitation. Writing the limitations yourself beats being caught.
Conclusion = abstract in different clothes: S1 phenomenon recast as recall; S2 tension deleted; S3 tense changed to did; S5 mechanism and S7 verification deleted; S6 numbers halved; S8 significance enlarged; new S9 next-stage sentence. Five-sentence skeleton: recall (we showed ...) -> contribution recalled with scope words first -> core finding (1-2 numbers with protocol) -> significance enlarged to "what should the field do differently" -> two or three concrete next directions. Similarity check against the abstract: no run of 8 identical words.
