# Introduction

Six paragraphs, each with one function, each ending on a sentence that narrows toward the unknown:
P1 territory (end on an unknown) -> P2 niche (the failure made specific: when, in what condition, what is missing) -> P3 occupy the niche and name the idea -> P4 mechanism, with the teaser figure as the toy example -> P5 two to four nearest neighbours and the mechanism-level difference -> P6 contributions (each maps to one figure or table) + wider significance.
When the problem's importance is not obvious, open with the deployment-level importance, then show the failure, then the solution.

Checklist: every paragraph ends on the unknown and narrows; the gap sentence is judgeable on importance alone; "what is new and what is not" is explicit; the last paragraph does not repeat the abstract's context and points to figures; contribution bullets map to figures or tables and carry the headline result only, never ablation numbers; every argument step has at least one citation; only nearest neighbours are discussed; terms are defined where first used; no internal experiment ids; Figure 1 states the claim on its own; the first sentence's domain assumption matches the target reviewers.
Acceptance test: give the first two paragraphs to someone who does not know the project and ask whether the gap matters.

## Patterns and templates
- The introduction is the expansion of three moves: territory -> niche (counter-claim / indicate a gap / raise a question / continue a tradition) -> occupy. It has four jobs the abstract cannot do: draw the novelty boundary, define terms, position nearest neighbours at mechanism level, list contributions. Toy example or Figure 1 lives here.
- Division with the abstract: context appears once (P1) here but is required in the abstract; method detail is one sentence there and P3-P4 here; results are one headline group there and several, more specific, figure-referenced groups here; terms are defined here; related work is zero citations there and a minimal neighbour set here.
- Niche sentences by archetype: capability = a question form ("we ask whether X can be solved directly") plus partial prior attempts; paradigm = "Unlike ..." with a gap of principle; mechanism = a dual tension (each of two families has one causal defect) -> "can they be unified / why does it fail".
- Anti-patterns: a broad literature review inside the intro, a buried lede, a gap that needs technical background to judge, unmotivated assumptions that readers care, terms coined mid-text, experiment ids in the text, a last paragraph that repeats the abstract's context.
