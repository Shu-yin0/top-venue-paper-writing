# Related work

- Group by relationship to the problem, not by chronology. Four relations must each be handled: same problem/different method; same method/different problem; similar method for a similar problem; neighbouring problem families.
- Each paragraph ends on a differentiating sentence ("Unlike X, which ..., we ..."). No list paragraphs: three consecutive sentences without a comparison is a violation.
- Every baseline used in the experiments appears here and is discussed. Cite the strongest form of the opposing view (no strawman). Tone: security, not competition.
- Keep only the minimal nearest neighbours; point to a survey for the rest. Division of labour with the intro: the intro states the key neighbours' difference briefly, this section gives the full map.
- Every citation has a function; no pile-ups. Colliding or same-name works are handled explicitly.
- Missing-citation check over the last two years of top venues. Verify each entry against a primary source (arXiv, publisher page, DBLP) before citing; a mismatching or invented entry can be a desk reject.
- Self-test: reading only the first and last sentence of each paragraph recovers "field map + where this paper sits".

## Patterns and templates
Paragraph template (a mini gap-statement): topic sentence (what this group of work does) -> representatives trimmed to the dimension that matters -> differentiating sentence ("Unlike X, which ___, our approach ___"). Prefer mechanism-level contrast axes (input representation, what is supervised, trainable parameters, deployment rule) to weak "different data" contrasts. When neighbours are dense, a difference table (their claim / our must-have difference) is the strongest anti-collision tool. Use an outsourcing sentence for large background: "We refer readers to [..] for in-depth reviews of ___". Cite broadly, discuss selectively. Start the search from the experiment's baselines and walk the citation graph.
Anti-patterns: list paragraphs, annotated-bibliography style, strawman comparison, missing near-neighbours from the last two years, putting others down, citations without a function, fighting the intro for content.
